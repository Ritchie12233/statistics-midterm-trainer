Tidy Tabular 
Thinking
• The Data Table: The fundamental object for representing datasets.
• Rows = Cases (Individuals): Each row contains all information 
about a single entry (e.g., one person, one ice cream cone, one 
movie).
• Columns = Variables (Attributes):  Each column describes a single 
aspect across all entries.
• Python implementation: We use the Table class from the 
datascience library or DataFrame from Pandas
Dr Hamza Altarturi

--- PAGE 12 ---
Classifying 
Variable Types
• EDA Rules: Match the chart and the statistic 
to the variable type.
• Categorical Variables: Groupings or labels.
• Nominal: No logical order (e.g., Ice 
cream flavor, State)
• Ordinal: Logical rank (e.g., Income level 
1 to 14)
• Numerical (Quantitative) Variables: 
Measured numbers with meaningful 
magnitudes
• Discrete: Specific jumps/counts (e.g., 
Number of movies, parity)
• Continuous: Any value in a range (e.g., 
Weight, Height, Salary)

The Effect of Shape, Skewness, & Outliers
Symmetrical: Mean 
and Median are roughly 
equal (bell curve / 
normal distribution)
Outliers: Extreme 
quantities that fall far 
outside the general 
scope of the data
Skewness: When data 
is asymmetrical, with a 
long tail extending in 
one direction
The Rule: For highly 
skewed data, the 
median is safer
Dr Hamza Altarturi


--- PAGE 21 ---
Summaries of 
One Variable 
Spread
How much does the data vary around the 
center?
Variance (.var()): The mean of the squared 
deviations from the center
Standard Deviation (.std()): The square root 
of variance. Measures the "typical" distance 
from the mean in original units
Informally: Values within 1 or 2 standard 
deviations from the mean are common; 
farther values are rare
Dr Hamza Altarturi


--- PAGE 22 ---
Spread: 
Percentiles 
and IQR
Percentiles (Quantiles): The value below 
which a certain percentage of data falls.
• Example: 25th percentile (Q1) and 75th 
percentile (Q3).
Interquartile Range (IQR): Q3 - Q1.
Meaning: The IQR measures the spread 
of the middle 50% of the data
Like the median, the IQR is highly 
resistant to extreme outliers.
Dr Hamza Altarturi

The Danger of Histogram Counts
• It is common to use raw counts on 
the y-axis of a histogram.
• The Problem: If bins are unequal in 
width, plotting raw counts 
drastically misrepresents the data
• A wide bin will artificially inflate the 
count, making it appear as a huge 
peak in the data and entirely 
obscuring the true shape of the 
distribution.
Dr Hamza Altarturi


--- PAGE 28 ---
The Area Principle & Density Scale
• The Area Principle: When representing magnitude in 2D, 
the area of the figure should represent the magnitude
• Density Scale: The vertical axis of a histogram must 
represent density (crowdedness), not counts
• Formula: Area of bar = Height × Width = Percent of entries 
in bin
• Height = Percent in bin / Width of bin
• Python Requirement: Always use plt.hist(..., 
density=True)
Dr Hamza Altarturi


--- PAGE 29 ---
Continuous 
Distributions 
& Density 
Curves
• As sample size increases and bin 
widths become extremely slim, the 
histogram approaches a smooth 
curve
• Probability Density Function: A 
smooth continuous curve where the 
total area under the curve is exactly 1
• Probabilities are calculated as the 
area under the curve between two 
points
• Note: The probability of hitting one 
exact continuous number (like exactly 
180.0000 cm) is mathematically zero. 
We measure intervals
Dr Hamza Altarturi

Spotting Modality and Patterns
• Histograms help us spot Modality (number of peaks).
• Unimodal: One clear peak.
• Bimodal: Two clear peaks.
• Why it matters: Bimodality often indicates the presence of two 
distinct sub-groups mixed together in your dataset.

--- PAGE 32 ---
Identifying Outliers Visually (IQR Method)
• Box plots automatically identify and flag outliers using the 1.5 × 
IQR Rule
• IQR = Q3−Q1.
• Fences:
• Upper Fence = Q3+1.5×IQR
• Lower Fence = Q1−1.5×IQR
• Any data point outside these fences is drawn as a distinct dot (an 
outlier)
Dr Hamza Altarturi


--- PAGE 33 ---
Handling 
Outliers
Once detected, what do we do with outliers?
Investigate: Are they data entry errors? (e.g., a 
pregnancy length of 50 weeks or a birth weight of 51 
lbs)
Clean: Errors should be filtered out or replaced with 
NaN (np.nan)
Retain: Some outliers are legitimate rare events. 
Removing true data without justification introduces 
bias.
Adapt: Use robust statistics (Median, IQR) that are 
not thrown off by legitimate outliers
Dr Hamza Altarturi

Discovering Relationships (Scatter Plots)

variables
• One point per row/case
• Explanatory vs. Response:
• x-axis: Explanatory (Predictor) variable.
• y-axis: Response (Outcome) variable.
• Python: plt.scatter(x_col, y_col)
Dr Hamza Altarturi


--- PAGE 37 ---
Spotting Direction, Form, and Strength
• When reading a scatter plot, evaluate:
• Direction:
• Positive: As X increases, Y increases
• Negative: As X increases, Y decreases
• Form: Linear (straight line trend) vs. Non-linear.
• Strength: How tightly clustered the points are around the invisible trend 
line.
• Outliers: Points deviating far from the general pattern (e.g., High average 
revenue but very low number of movies)
Dr Hamza Altarturi


--- PAGE 38 ---
Cross-Classifying Categorical Variables
• How do we find relationships between two categorical variables?
• Contingency Tables / Pivot Tables: Group together rows that share 
a combination of values and organize the counts into a grid
• Python: pd.crosstab(df['col1'], df['col2']) or df.pivot()
• Can be adapted to find the sum or mean of a third numerical 
variable within that gri
Dr Hamza Altarturi


--- PAGE 39 ---
Comparing Groups Visually
• Overlaid Graphs: We can plot distributions for different groups on 
the exact same axes to compare them directly.
• Techniques:
• Overlaid Histograms: Plotting two density histograms on top of each other 
(using transparency/alpha) to see the shift in center and spread
• Overlaid Scatter Plots: Using different colors to represent a third 
categorical variable on a 2D scatter plot
• Side-by-side Box Plots: Plotting a numerical variable grouped by a 
categorical variable
Dr Hamza Altarturi
