What is 
Probability?
• Random Process: A process (like rolling a 
die or flipping a coin) that gives rise to an 
outcome we cannot perfectly predict.
• Probability: The proportion of times an 
outcome would occur if we observed the 
random process an infinite number of 
times.
• Law of Large Numbers: As more 
observations are collected, the proportion 
of occurrences with a particular outcome 
converges to the true probability.

Sample Spaces and Disjoint Outcomes
Sample Space (): The set of all possible outcomes for a random 
process (e.g., for a die).Sample
Event: A subset or collection of outcomes from the sample space (e.g., 
rolling an even number).Event
Disjoint (Mutually Exclusive) Outcomes: Two outcomes that cannot 
both happen at the same time (e.g., rolling a 1 and a 2 on a single die).Disjoint
Dr Hamza Altarturi


--- PAGE 6 ---
The Addition Rule
If all outcomes are equally likely, P(Event) = (# outcomes that make event happen)/(# all 
outcomes).
Addition Rule for Disjoint Outcomes: If events and are disjoint, the probability that either 
occurs is their sum.
P(A or B)=P(A)+P(B)
Complement: The probability of an event not happening
The complement rule is powerful: P(not A) = 1 − P(A).
Often easier to compute the complement first.
Dr Hamza Altarturi


--- PAGE 7 ---
Independent Processes & The Multiplication Rule
Independent Processes: Knowing the outcome of 
one provides no useful information about the 
outcome of the other (e.g., rolling two separate dice).
Multiplication Rule for Independent Processes: If 
and are independent, the probability that both occur 
is their product.
For independent events, P(A and B)=P(A)×P(B)
Dr Hamza Altarturi

Bayes' 
Theorem and 
Bayesian 
Intuition
• The Monty Hall problem is an exercise in 
updating probabilities based on new 
information.
• The Bayesian Approach:
o Prior belief: We believe is some value 
before seeing data.
o Observe data: A conditional event occurs.
o Posterior belief: We update our belief based 
on the data using Bayes' rule.
• Instead of thinking of parameters as fixed 
unknowns, we model our belief about 
them using probability distributions.
Dr Hamza Altarturi

Probability 
Mass 
Functions 
(PMFs)
PMF: A function that represents a 
discrete distribution by mapping each 
possible quantity to its probability
Normalized: The sum of all 
probabilities in a PMF always exactly 
equals 1.0
By dividing frequencies (counts) by 
the total sample size n, we turn raw 
counts into probabilities
Dr Hamza Altarturi

The Class 
Size 
Paradox
• The Paradox
• The university reports an average class 
size of 24. However, surveying students 
reveals an average class size of 29
• Why?
• Large classes have more students to 
observe them. A class of 50 has 50 
students reporting it; a class of 5 has 
only 5 students reporting it
• This is the Inspection Paradox
• The act of observing the data biases the 
sample
Dr Hamza Altarturi
