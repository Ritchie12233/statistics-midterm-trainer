Parameters, Statistics, and Inference
• Parameter
• A measure concerning a whole 
population (e.g., the true average of the 
population).
• Statistic
• A measure concerning a sample (e.g., the 
sample average).
• Inferential Statistics
• Using data from an observed sample to 
make a conclusion about a broader 
population.



--- PAGE 7 ---
The Danger of Anecdotal 
Evidence
• Anecdotal Evidence: Data collected 
informally or haphazardly, usually based on 
personal, unpublished stories.
• Why it fails:
• Small sample size: Fails to capture 
natural variation.
• Selection & Confirmation Bias: People 
share and remember stories that 
confirm their existing beliefs.
• Inaccuracy: Human memory is flawed.

--- PAGE 9 ---
Other 
Sources of 
Bias
• Non-Response Bias: Occurs when individuals who choose not 
to participate differ significantly from those who do.
• Response Bias: Occurs when participants do not respond 
truthfully (often to appease the researcher or fit social norms).


--- PAGE 10 ---
Probability-
Based Sampling
Simple Random 
Sample (SRS)
SRS: Every member of the 
population has an equal 
chance of being selected
This is the gold standard for 
avoiding sampling bias
Operates like drawing 
names from a well-mixed 
hat


--- PAGE 11 ---
Advanced 
Sampling
Stratified Sampling
Stratified Sampling: A "divide-
and-conquer" strategy.
The population is divided into 
similar groups (strata).
A simple random sample is then 
taken within each stratum.
Goal: To ensure every sub-
group is adequately 
represented.


--- PAGE 12 ---
Advanced 
Sampling 
Cluster & Multistage
Cluster Sampling: The population is 
broken into many diverse groups 
(clusters). A few clusters are 
randomly selected, and all cases in 
those clusters are sampled.
Multistage Sampling: Similar to 
cluster, but a random sample is taken 
within the selected clusters instead 
of measuring everyone.
Goal: To save time and money when 
populations are spread out.

The

• Confounding Variable: A third, 
unmeasured variable that is related 
to both the explanatory and 
response variables.
• Also known as a "lurking variable. "
• The Rule: Association does NOT 
imply causation!

Principles of Experimental Design
Controlling: Standardizing 
differences across groups 
(e.g., all patients take the 
pill with 12oz of water)
Randomization: Randomly 
assigning cases to 
treatments
Replication: Collecting a 
sufficiently large sample to 
accurately estimate the 
effect
Blocking: Grouping 
individuals by a known 
variable before 
randomizing

--- PAGE 19 ---
Blinding
Single-Blind Study
• The participants do not know which 
treatment group they are in
Double-Blind Study
• Neither the participants nor the 
researchers interacting with them know 
who is in which group.
Goal
• To prevent researcher bias and participant 
placebo effects.


--- PAGE 20 ---
Summary
• Data Collection is Critical: A study is only as good as its sample.
• Avoid Bias: Use probability-based sampling (Simple Random, 
Stratified) over convenience sampling and anecdotes.
• Observational Studies: Reveal associations; they are vulnerable 
to confounding variables.
• Randomized Experiments: Can establish causation by 
controlling the environment and utilizing randomization, 
placebos, and blinding.

Why Program in Data Science?
• The Problem: Computers cannot study the 
world on their own.
• The Solution: People must precisely describe 
what steps the computer should take to 
collect and analyze data. These steps are 
expressed through programs.
• Why Python?
• Python 3 is a widely used language in 
commercial applications, scientific 
experiments, and open-source projects.
• Allows us to handle diverse datasets (text, 
images, video) beyond just numbers.

--- PAGE 25 ---
Core Python 
Libraries
• Libraries: Toolkits created by the community 
that add specific capabilities to Python.
• Data scientists rely on three foundational 
libraries:
• numpy (imported as np): Used for fast 
numerical computation.
• pandas (imported as pd): Used for 
managing and analyzing tabular data.

--- PAGE 26 ---
The Pandas 
DataFrame
• DataFrame: The Pandas 
data structure that 
represents tabular data.
• It organizes data into 
• Rows (cases/observations) 
• Columns (variables).
• Index: The left-most 
column that contains a 
label for each row (usually 
integers starting from 0).


--- PAGE 27 ---
Loading 
Data into 
Python
• Before analysis, data must be loaded into the 
Python environment.
• Pandas provides methods to read various file 
formats directly into a DataFrame.
• pd.read_csv(): Reads standard comma-
separated files.
• pd.read_fwf(): Reads fixed-width formatted 
data files.
• We assign the loaded data to a variable name 
(e.g., preg = read_stata(...)).


--- PAGE 28 ---
Inspecting 
the 
DataFrame
Once loaded, always inspect your data to 
ensure it was imported correctly!
shape: An attribute that returns the number 
of rows and columns.
head(): A method that displays the first 5 
rows of the DataFrame.
columns: An attribute that returns an Index 
object containing all variable names.


--- PAGE 29 ---
The Pandas Series
• Series: A Pandas data structure representing a single column of 
data (a sequence of values).
• Extracted using brackets: pregordr = preg["pregordr"].
• Series also have a dtype (data type):
• int64: 64-bit integers (whole numbers).
• float64: Floating-point values (decimals).

Data Validation: Comparing basic

--- PAGE 31 ---
Handling 
Missing 
Data
Missing Data: Real-world data often has 
inapplicable or refused answers.
NaN (Not a Number): How Pandas 
represents missing values.
Use value_counts(dropna=False) to see 
the count of NaN values.
replace(): Method used to overwrite 
error codes (e.g., 97, 98, 99) with np.nan.


--- PAGE 32 ---
Data Transformation
Transformation: Converting data into different formats or 
performing arithmetic calculations across columns.
Recodes: New variables calculated based on the raw data.
Examples of mathematical 
operations in Pandas:
Division: preg["agepreg"] /= 100.0
Addition across columns: preg["totalwgt_lb"] = 
preg["birthwgt_lb"] + preg["birthwgt_oz"] / 16.0

Summary 
Statistics in 
Python
• Pandas Series provide built-in 
methods to compute basic 
summary statistics:
• count(): Returns the number of 
non-NaN values.
• sum(): Adds up all values.
• mean(): Calculates the average.
• var(): Computes the variance.
• std(): Computes the standard 
deviation.


--- PAGE 35 ---
Exploring 
Subsets of 
Data (query)
• query(): A Pandas method used to 
filter a DataFrame based on specific 
conditions.
• Takes a string containing column 
names and comparison operators.
• Example: subset = 
preg.query("caseid == 10229")
• Returns a smaller DataFrame 
containing only the rows where the 
condition is True.
