Why Use Theoretical Models?
Abstractions: Theoretical 
models leave out idiosyncrasies 
and measurement errors to 
capture the true underlying 
signal
Data Compression: A massive 
dataset can be summarized 
mathematically by just one or 
two parameters
Mathematical Insight: Natural 
phenomena often fit theoretical 
distributions, revealing 
underlying physical truths (e.g., 
additive vs. proportionate 
growth)
Caveat: All models are 
imperfect. Real-world data is 
never generated by a perfect 
mathematical equation

--- PAGE 5 ---
Mathematical Properties of the Normal Curve
•Symmetry: The density function is symmetric about x=μ, 
meaning f(μ+x)=f(μ−x).
•The mean and median are mathematically identical.
•Maximum Density: f(x) hits its absolute maximum at x=μ.
•Inflection Points: The curve changes from concave-down to 
concave-up at exactly x=μ+σ and x=μ−σ

The Empirical 
Rule 
(68-95-99.7%)
•Because the area under the normal curve is 
exactly 1, we can define strict mathematical 
boundaries:
•68% of the data falls within ±1σ (z between -1 and 1)
•95% of the data falls within ±2σ (z between -2 and 2)
•99.73% of the data falls within ±3σ.
•Values beyond 3σ are statistically highly 
unusual.

methods

Distribution Function (CDF)

When Data is 
NOT Normal
• Many natural datasets exhibit skewness and fail 
the Q-Q plot test (e.g., forming a curved line 
instead of a straight line)
• Lognormal Distribution: If changes in a variable 
are proportionate rather than additive, the data 
will skew right
• Solution: Taking the logarithm (log 10) of the data 
will transform it into a perfectly normal 
distribution
• Conclusion: It is fine if data isn't normal. We 
simply apply a different mathematical model

The Central Limit Theorem (CLT)
• The Statement: The probability distribution of the sum or average 
of a large random sample will be roughly normal, regardless of 
the distribution of the population from which the sample is drawn
• Mathematical Notation: As n→∞,
• Rule of Thumb: A sample size of n≥30 is usually sufficient for the 
CLT to apply and for the curve to appear normal
